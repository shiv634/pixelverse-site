What Volsplat does

Volsplat turns real or computer-made views of a subject into a Gaussian splat: a 3D scene made of many soft, semi-transparent 3D blobs (Gaussians) that you can orbit and look at from any angle. It runs on Windows, trains on your NVIDIA graphics card, and lets you clean the result up and take it to other tools, including Unreal Engine.

You can start from:

  • photos from a phone or camera,
  • video files (mp4, mov, avi and similar),
  • one or more live webcams,
  • renders from a 3D program such as Blender, Houdini or Maya, where the cameras are already known,
  • a 3D model (obj, stl, ply or glb),
  • a single picture,
  • or a splat you already have (a standard .ply file).

Before you start

Computer
Windows, 64-bit. Windows can install WSL2 (Ubuntu) for you during setup, and Windows may ask you to restart once.
Graphics card
An NVIDIA card. Training runs on CUDA inside WSL2. Volsplat is developed and timed on an RTX 5070 Ti with 16 GB, and the highest quality level needs most of a 16 GB card. Minimum requirements for smaller cards have not been measured yet.
Disk space
Plan for tens of gigabytes: the 3D engine (CUDA, PyTorch), the optional AI models, and your scans. Single image input alone needs about 15 GB free inside WSL. A highest-quality scan can be about 700 MB.
Internet
Needed for the one-time setup downloads. After that, scanning and training work offline. Activating a licence and checking for updates need the internet.

Install and first run

  1. Download the installer from the Volsplat page and run it. The beta installer is not code-signed yet, so Windows may show a SmartScreen warning: choose More info, then Run anyway.
  2. Start VolSpatial SplatScan. On the first run it asks whether you want to send an anonymous usage note. Nothing is sent before you answer, and nothing is sent if you say no. You can change this later in Preferences or Help > Privacy and your data.
  3. Your workspace is the folder C:\Users\<you>\VolSpatial SplatScan. It holds your scans (captures), your preferences, the log, and a copy of the engine files that the GPU side runs. File > Open my SplatScan folder opens it.

The basic workflow

  1. Create a project (Ctrl+N).
  2. Bring in your pictures with the Import and Capture panels.
  3. In the Process tab, pick how the cameras are found, choose a quality level, and press Process.
  4. Look at the result in the 3D view and level the scene if needed.
  5. Tidy it: crop, remove floaters, delete unwanted splats.
  6. Export a .ply or send it to Unreal Engine.

The window

The left side has the Project, Recent, Import, Cameras and Capture panels. The right side has the Scene, Process, Preferences and Log tabs. The 3D view is in the middle. Panels can be docked left or right, floated as their own window, snapped to edges or hidden. View > Reset layout puts everything back.

Bringing in pictures

Photos

File > Import photos (Ctrl+I) takes a folder of photos from a phone, DSLR or similar.

Video files

Import video file(s) reads mp4, mov, avi and similar and uses frames from them. Use video file(s) as cameras treats several videos as several cameras. For handheld or phone video, keep rolling-shutter compensation on in the training settings.

Webcams

Press Detect to find webcams and see a preview. Capture round (Space) takes one picture from every camera; Auto every repeats it. Each physical camera gets its own folder, so one webcam keeps one set of lens settings while you move it around the scene.

A COLMAP model you already have

Import sparse model (COLMAP sparse/0) brings in cameras solved by COLMAP, Metashape or RealityCapture exports.

A splat you already have

Open splat file (.ply) (Ctrl+O) opens a standard Gaussian splat .ply, such as one written by gsplat, Postshot or SuperSplat.

Finding the cameras

Before training, Volsplat must know where each picture was taken from. In the Process tab under Camera positions there are three routes:

RouteUse it whenNotes
COLMAP (accurate, slower)Normal photos and videoThe Accuracy setting has three levels. Fast reads photos at 1600 px with a lighter solve (seconds instead of minutes). High works like a photogrammetry tool: more features, matching that rescues weak pairs, and lens distortion solved with the cameras. It takes about twice as long.
AI guess (fast)You want cameras in one quick passNeeds SETUP_AI.bat. A neural network predicts every camera in one pass. You can tick Polish the AI guess with COLMAP for a more precise result a few minutes later.
Imported cameras + depth (exact, fastest)The pictures are renders from Blender, Houdini, Maya or similarReads transforms.json (nerfstudio, instant-ngp, Blender-NeRF) or a COLMAP text model. Depth maps (such as .exr) are optional but recommended, and let the splats start on the true surface so fewer training steps are needed.

For computer-made renders with a checkerboard floor or a plain sky, tick CG render: ignore checkerboard floor and sky. A checker floor makes frames match the wrong squares; the mask only affects camera solving, and training still uses the whole picture.

After the cameras are found, Volsplat works out where the floor is, which way is up, and a real-world size, using the cameras first, then a flat plane, then the shape of the point cloud. Level the floor improves this using the trained splat.

Other options

  • Method: MCMC (a capped splat count, the default) or Classic (adaptive splat count).
  • Spherical harmonics (0 to 3): how much view-dependent colour, such as shine and reflections. 0 is plain colour and the smallest; 3 is best.
  • Anti-aliasing: off in every preset. In testing, a long run with anti-aliasing and a large splat cap collapsed, with most splats ending up transparent.
  • Photometric compensation: copes with exposure changes between photos.
  • Depth guidance (on by default): uses depth from the AI or the camera solve to guide training.
  • Rolling-shutter compensation (on by default): lets each frame’s camera settle during training, which helps handheld and phone video.
  • Fill holes: adds new splats where the model left a gap in the surface. Gaps on a real depth edge, such as railings or the space between two objects, are left alone.

When training ends, Result shows the output. The scan lives in your workspace as a folder containing scene.ply, plus timings.json showing where the time went. Retrain with these settings and Reset field (train from scratch) are in the Radiance field section.

Looking at and fixing the result

The 3D view

Left drag rotates, right or middle drag (or Shift + left drag) pans, and the wheel zooms. Real splat drawing (GPU) draws true Gaussians; otherwise a fast point preview is used. Hide stray floaters, a ground grid, an axis gizmo and a statistics overlay are in the View menu. View result as real splats (browser) opens the result in a browser viewer.

Scene tools

  • Transform: position, rotation and scale. Bake into data writes it into scene.ply.
  • Crop box: hides everything outside (or inside) a box. Drag a coloured square to push a face in or out, or the white diamond to move the box. The crop changes only what you see and export; the trained data is untouched.
  • Optimise now: removes floaters and tidies (drops transparent, oversized and isolated splats). Tidy automatically after every scan does it for you.
  • Undo and redo cover transforms, the crop box, the up axis and deletions in one timeline.

Pick and delete splats

Choose Brush, Lasso or Box on the left (or B, L, M). Picked splats turn orange. Press Delete to delete them. Save writes the edit into scene.ply, and the file before your first saved edit is kept as scene_before_edit.ply. The SuperSplat web editor can also be opened from the Process tab.

Lighting

A splat has no lights: the colour it was photographed with is baked in, so it cannot be relit afterwards. When you convert a 3D model, the Lighting controls (Light, Turn, Exposure) set the light before the views are made, so the baked result is lit the way you want.

Other ways to make a splat

Single image

Needs SETUP_SINGLE_IMAGE.bat. It works in two steps so you can judge the shape before committing: 1. Create 3D model makes a mesh you can turn in the 3D view; the model guesses the sides it cannot see, and Variation gives a different guess. 2. Convert to Gaussian splat turns the approved model into a splat. The result is saved as a scan of its own. As an online alternative you can use Meshy with your own API key: Volsplat never holds an account for you, and the key is stored in one local file and masked everywhere it is shown.

A 3D model

Import 3D model opens obj, stl, ply or glb. Convert model to Gaussian splat renders it from a ring of viewpoints whose positions are known exactly, so there is no camera solving to go wrong, then trains with the imported cameras.

Near-live splat

With cameras attached, Volsplat can rebuild a splat from the newest frames every few seconds without training, using the AI model (needs SETUP_AI.bat). It is rougher than a trained splat but exists seconds after the cameras start. Controls include rounds per second, how many recent frames to keep, a point budget, and AI clean-up + steady map, which removes floaters and edge smear and averages every update into one steady map. Use as project scene keeps what you see.

Unreal Engine

Volsplat ships prebuilt Unreal plugins for Unreal Engine 5.6, 5.7 and 5.8: NanoGS, which draws the splat, and VolSplatBridge, which imports what you send. No compiler or Visual Studio is needed.

Set up Unreal

Choose Set up Unreal Engine. Volsplat finds the Unreal installs on your computer and shows which it can set up. Install into one project, or into the engine to cover every project of that Unreal version (this may need Volsplat run as administrator).

Send a splat

Choose Send to Unreal Engine, pick the folder that contains your .uproject file, give the splat a name and press Send. Volsplat stands the scene upright, scales it to metres, converts it to Unreal’s axes and units, and writes it into the project’s SplatScanInbox folder. The bridge plugin imports it as a saved asset, and Volsplat is not needed afterwards.

Options include: size multiplier, turn about the vertical, stand it on the floor and centre it at the origin, leave out faint splats (less ghosting), the spherical-harmonics degree (0 to 3), a cap on the splat count, brightness, replacing the splat already in the level, and also placing it in the open level.

  • Proxy mesh: a hidden blocky stand-in with the shape of the scan. The splat is still the only thing you see, but the proxy lets it cast a shadow and be clicked in Unreal.
  • Relightable: Unreal cannot light a splat by itself because its colour is baked in. With the separate StarLightGS plugin in your project, Volsplat can add a surface direction to every splat (taken from the model it was made from, so it works for splats converted from a model) so Unreal’s lights can shade it.

Exporting

Export field (Ctrl+E) writes a standard 3D Gaussian splat .ply with your transform and crop applied, which other tools that read this format can open. Save 3D view as a picture is Ctrl+P. Without a licence these exports are locked; see below.

Beta, licences and updates

This beta

The beta unlocks every feature until {EXPIRES} and needs no licence key and no server. After that date it locks the same features an unlicensed copy locks and tells you where to get the current build. It never deletes anything and never stops you opening your own scans.

An unlicensed copy locks four things: exporting a .ply, Send to Unreal, saving the 3D view as a picture, and sharing (opening the result in the browser viewer or revealing scene.ply). Everything else, including capture, camera solving, training, viewing and editing, stays available.

Licences at launch

Planned licences are Indie, Perpetual and Commercial (see the Volsplat page); for now all unlock every feature. You activate a copy with Help > Licence by typing your key. The key is checked once, then the licence is stored on your PC and works offline for up to 30 days; the app renews it quietly in the background when it is a few days old. Deactivate frees the PC slot, for example before moving to a new computer. A licence for a given computer can also be loaded from a file.

Updates

Help > Check for updates asks pixelversefx.com whether a newer version exists. The answer is digitally signed, and the downloaded installer must match a published checksum, so a broken or tampered download is deleted without running. During the beta the update service may not be online yet, in which case the app says it cannot reach the update server. Install new versions from the download section.

Third-party software

Volsplat is built on open-source software, and each part keeps its own licence. Shipped inside the Windows program: Python, Tcl/Tk, OpenCV (with FFmpeg for reading video), NumPy, Pillow and the PyInstaller bootloader. Downloaded onto your PC during setup, inside WSL: gsplat, PyTorch, the NVIDIA CUDA Toolkit, COLMAP / pycolmap and their helper packages, and, only if you install them, fused-ssim, MapAnything (Apache-2.0 weights only) and TRELLIS (MIT).

Models with non-commercial licences are deliberately not used. The full list with licence names and links is in THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.txt, which sits in your workspace folder and inside the install folder.

When something goes wrong

  • Open the Log tab: it shows what each stage did. File > Open my SplatScan folder opens the scans, preferences and log on disk.
  • If a setup stops with an error, run Help > Set up / repair the 3D engine again, and keep the text from the setup window to send us.
  • If training is slow or the card runs out of memory, choose a lower quality level, a smaller picture size, or a lower splat cap.
  • A soft result usually means the cameras were slightly off. Try COLMAP with High accuracy, or Polish the AI guess with COLMAP.
  • Still stuck? Contact the studio and tell us your Windows version, graphics card and what the Log tab says.
